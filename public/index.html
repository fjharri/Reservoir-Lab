01 / HYDRAULIC VALVE CONDITION

Quick Comparison
ESN vs GRU

This experiment uses a balanced 640-cycle subset of the Condition monitoring of hydraulic systems dataset. From the full 2,205-cycle dataset, it retains six raw sensor channels and asks both models to classify one target: valve condition across four degradation states. Every 60-second cycle is reduced to 120 time steps, and all training runs locally on this device.

PROTOCOL DETAILS

What runs
on the device.

Input and split

Each example is a 60-second hydraulic cycle represented by 120 half-second averages from six sensors. The fixed split contains 400 training cycles, 120 validation cycles and 120 test cycles, with equal numbers from the four valve states. Sensor scaling is calculated from the training split and then reused for validation and test data.

Models

At the default settings, the ESN has 500 fixed reservoir neurons and fits a 2,004-parameter linear readout by ridge regression. The GRU has 16 recurrent units and 1,172 trainable parameters, optimized in batches of eight with Adam. Both models average their 120 recurrent states before producing a four-class prediction.

Timing

ESN training time covers reservoir features for the training cycles, feature standardisation and the ridge solve. GRU training time covers model and training-tensor setup plus completed gradient updates. Data loading, validation, test inference, chart updates and pauses between batches are outside both training clocks.

Checkpoint selection

The experiment saves the last complete GRU update within the ESN training time and the first checkpoint that reaches the ESN validation target. Training then continues until six validation checks show no improvement, or until the time cap. The finalised GRU restores the checkpoint with the highest validation accuracy.
